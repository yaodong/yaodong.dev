AI can propose a solution, write the code, and turn a complex workflow into a series of yes-or-no answers. Checking its work still takes judgment, and I've been thinking about where that judgment comes from.

Recently an AI agent wrote a small pull request for me, and it took me a whole day to find the one corner case it had missed. What let me find it was years of doing the work myself, writing code, getting it wrong, and fixing it. Confirming finished answers builds none of that. When my only role is to say yes or no, I'm never wrong in a way I can feel, and nothing sticks.

So keeping that understanding has become deliberate practice for me. I work a problem before asking, write ideas in my own words, and read code I don't strictly have to read. Little of it is required to finish the task, but it's the part that made finding that corner case possible.
