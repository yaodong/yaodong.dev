Finalize the blog post at: $ARGUMENTS

## Context

The author is a non-native English speaker. The writing should be correct, clear, and natural, but not overly sophisticated. Avoid rare vocabulary or complex idioms that would feel unnatural for a second-language writer.

## Instructions

1. Read the specified blog post file
2. Check for errors and unnatural expressions
3. Suggest corrections that maintain the author's authentic voice
4. Keep the language accessible and straightforward

## Check For

### Spelling and Typos

- Misspelled words
- Common typos (teh → the, recieve → receive)
- Inconsistent spelling (color vs colour — pick one)

### Grammar Errors

- Subject-verb agreement
- Tense consistency
- Article usage (a/an/the) — common challenge for non-native speakers
- Preposition errors (depend on, consist of, etc.)
- Plural/singular confusion
- Run-on sentences and fragments

### Unnatural Expressions

- Direct translations from other languages that sound awkward in English
- Word order that follows non-English patterns
- Phrases that are grammatically correct but not how native speakers would say it
- Overly literal expressions

### Cultural and Idiomatic Issues

- Idioms used incorrectly or in wrong contexts
- Cultural references that may not translate well
- Expressions that might confuse English-speaking readers

### Clarity Issues

- Ambiguous pronoun references
- Unclear antecedents
- Sentences that require re-reading to understand
- Missing context that native speakers might assume

## Style Guidelines for Non-Native Writers

### Prefer

- Common, everyday vocabulary
- Short, clear sentences
- Simple verb tenses when possible
- Direct statements

### Avoid

- Rare or literary words (utilize → use, commence → start)
- Complex idioms or slang
- Overly formal academic language
- Phrasal verbs that might be misused
- Em-dashes (—) unless absolutely necessary; prefer commas, parentheses, colons, or separate sentences

## What NOT to Change

- The author's voice and personality
- Technical terms and jargon (these are intentional)
- Deliberately simple or direct phrasing
- Content and meaning

## Output Format

### Issues Found

For each issue, provide:
- **Original**: The problematic text (quoted)
- **Issue**: What's wrong (spelling/grammar/unnatural/unclear)
- **Correction**: The suggested fix
- **Explanation**: Brief note on why (helps the author learn)

### Summary

- Total issues found by category
- Overall assessment of the writing quality

### Finalized Version

Provide the complete corrected post (excluding frontmatter), ready for publication.
